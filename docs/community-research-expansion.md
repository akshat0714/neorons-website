# Community atlas expansion: four additional perspectives

Sources checked **November 14, 2026**. Public-ready short copy and optional context are in `work/community-expansion-research.json` in the workspace. No new headline mortality figures are recommended. The existing four perspectives remain documented in `community-research-sources.md`.

## Alaska Native communities — Alaska, United States

Use a broad regional label and identify **Yup’ik communities in southwest Alaska** when discussing Qungasvik. Do not extend that programme to every Alaska Native people. The rural-access evidence concerns Alaska generally.

- [University of Alaska Fairbanks: Qungasvik](https://www.uaf.edu/canhr/research/past-projects/qungasvik-toolbox-indigenous-intervention-science-model-alaska-native-communities.php) — primary programme account.
- [Statewide Suicide Prevention Plan 2023–2027](https://health.alaska.gov/media/zavdztbn/230301_stateplan_suicideprevention.pdf) — strategy 4.3, printed pages 26–27.

The proposed ANSEP photo is education in Anchorage. It does not identify participants as Yup’ik or show Qungasvik, clinical care, or Neorons activity.

## First Nations communities — Canada

The First Nations Health Authority provides a **British Columbia example**, while the wellness framework is national. These are distinct Nations and service arrangements. The evaluation period is 2015–16 to 2020–21; do not imply its findings are a current census of services.

- [First Nations Health Authority: Hope, Help and Healing](https://fnha.ca/services-and-support/preventative-care/mental-health-and-wellness/suicide-prevention/) — Indigenous health organisation’s planning resource.
- [First Nations Mental Wellness Continuum Framework](https://www.sac-isc.gc.ca/eng/1576093687903/1576093725971) — jointly developed policy foundation, December 2014.
- [Indigenous Services Canada: Phase One Evaluation](https://www.sac.gc.ca/eng/1748871901482/1748871945070) — source for continuing capacity, coordination and community-priority themes.

Photo captions should identify the documented Nation or event, without assuming involvement in the cited prevention work.

## Guarani communities — Brazil

The selected example is **Mbyá-Guarani work in coastal São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro**. It replaces the initially considered Guarani-Kaiowá example to align more closely with the available Mbyá school photograph. The school remains in a different state: Rio Grande do Sul.

- [OTSS project descriptions](https://www.otss.org.br/projetos-otss) — community/public-research partnership account; use the mental-health description near the bottom. Its municipality label for Sapukai is inconsistent, so retain the broader coastal geography.
- [OTSS: Integral health of traditional peoples and communities](https://www.otss.org.br/area-de-atuacao/saude-integral-pcts) — named leadership and programme places.
- [Rio de Janeiro State Health Secretariat and UERJ: Histórias da Atenção Psicossocial, 2025](https://www.rj.gov.br/saude/sites/default/files/arquivo_pagina_basica/HISTORIAS-DA-ATENCAO-PSICOSSOCIAL-NO-ESTADO-DO-RIO-DE-JANEIRO-compactado.pdf) — qualitative account titled *Ara Pyau: Tempo Bom*. It supports the suicide-prevention context, not a mortality rate or measured outcome.

Do not interchange Mbyá and Kaiowá. Do not describe the Kuaray Rete school photo as the coastal health initiative. Avoid copying descriptions of suicide methods from background sources.

## Maya communities — Guatemala

This is a **language-access and local-capacity perspective**, not evidence of an unusually high Maya-wide suicide rate. The documented work concerns COVID-19-era activities in Maya Mam-speaking San Marcos and Maya Ixil-speaking Quiché. No current nationwide coverage is established.

- [PAHO/WHO: Guatemala mental-health and psychosocial-support response](https://www.paho.org/en/responding-mental-health-and-psychosocial-support-mhpss-needs-during-covid-19-indigenous-and-afro-1).
- [PAHO end-of-biennium report](https://www.paho.org/sites/default/files/csp30-7-add-i-e-report-end-biennium_0.pdf) — corroborating Guatemala programme example.

The official indexed text was available for both PAHO sources; direct automated openings returned 403. Source copy describes language-specific training and outreach. Do not infer a photographed person’s Maya subgroup from clothing, a craft, or appearance. Preserve the image’s own location and activity description.

## Presentation boundaries

Keep the public cards short and place the longer context and sources in a disclosure. All numbers, dates and programme locations must retain their scope. Research references do not establish Neorons services, partnerships, supported languages, or referral routes. Photos document community life, not a person’s health status or a Neorons activity. No hotline numbers or direct hotline contact URLs are supplied. No numerical comparison between communities is proposed. The optional paragraphs are 60–100 words each and avoid claims of measured intervention efficacy.
