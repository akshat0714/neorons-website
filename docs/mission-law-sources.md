# Mission: Indigenous rights framework sources

Source check: **14 November 2026**. Research data: `work/mission-laws.json` in the workspace.

These entries describe **policy positions supporting implementation**, authorized by the user’s request for laws Neorons advocates for. They do not assert drafting, passage, negotiations, local delivery or authority to speak for Indigenous communities. No causal suicide-prevention outcomes are attributed to these instruments.

Use the JSON’s short public summaries. Keep jurisdiction and legal-status labels visible. These eight cards are a selected framework, not an exhaustive account of local law.

## Verification notes

- **UNDRIP:** resolution 61/295, adopted 13 September 2007. A General Assembly declaration, not a treaty. Distinguish its international status from related domestic obligations. The UN text was also cross-checked against the declaration reproduced in the schedule to Canada’s Act.
- **ILO 169:** adopted 27 June 1989; international entry into force 5 September 1991. NORMLEX lists Brazil (25 July 2002), Guatemala (5 June 1996) and Norway (19 June 1990) as ratified/in force. Australia, Canada, New Zealand and the United States are absent from its ratifiers. Consultation scope must not be represented as a universal veto.
- **Canada:** two separate statutes are grouped for presentation. Both official consolidations show currency through 19 October 2026 and amendments to 27 November 2024. The UN Declaration Act received assent 21 June 2021; the Indigenous Languages Act is S.C. 2019, c. 23.
- **United States:** the Native American Languages Act is 1990 federal legislation, now codified as amended in 25 U.S.C. chapter 31. The reviewed § 2903 version includes laws through 7 October 2026; § 2901 through 20 October 2026. Policy support must not become an unlimited service guarantee.
- **Norway:** the government’s English text is dated 2 July 2025 and includes the revised language categories and consultation chapter. Geographic/institutional limits matter; the Norwegian Act cannot be generalized across Sápmi.
- **Aotearoa New Zealand:** the official index explicitly marks Act 2016 No. 17 in force, latest version 5 April 2023. The Act continues official-language status and establishes Te Mātāwai; 2016 was not the first recognition of te reo Māori as official.
- **Brazil:** Articles 231 and 232 remain in the consolidated constitutional text. The card covers recognition and standing, not the outcome of individual demarcation disputes or current contested implementing legislation.
- **Guatemala:** the law text is published by the statutory Maya language academy; Congress publications from 2026 continue to describe and apply Decree 19-2003. Articles 14 and 15 are distinct: communication in public services, then prioritized sectors. Spanish remains official.

## Retrieval limitations

Some direct fetches of NORMLEX, New Zealand Legislation, Lovdata and UN pages returned redirect loops, 403/405 or timeout responses. Their current official indexed text and alternate official publications were available. Do not describe the research date as a guarantee that every consolidation was updated that day; the currency dates above are the dates actually exposed by the publishers.

Government and legislative texts were summarized in original short prose. The public copy uses no extended quotations. Source-specific derivation stays compact; do not expand cards by copying statutory wording.
