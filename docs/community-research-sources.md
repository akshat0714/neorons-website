# Community atlas: sources and editorial boundaries

Sources checked **November 14, 2026**. The Programmes atlas contains four regional research perspectives. They are not Neorons service locations, partnerships, or evidence that the phone service supports the communities’ languages. Coordinates in `js/communities-data.js` are approximate visual anchors, not community boundaries or health facilities.

## Inuit communities — Nunavut, Canada

**Anchor:** latitude 67.0, longitude −95.0.

The current context comes from the June 25, 2025 joint declaration by the Government of Nunavut and Nunavut Tunngavik Inc. It identifies a continuing suicide crisis and describes Inuit-led prevention through Inuusivut Anninaqtuq, involving community knowledge and young people. Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami’s strategy supports the separate point about unequal healthcare access and the importance of Inuit-specific mental-health support.

- [Government of Nunavut and NTI declaration, June 25, 2025](https://www.gov.nu.ca/en/newsroom/department-health-and-nunavut-tunngavik-inc-declare-suicide-continuing-crisis-nunavut-2025)
- [Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami: National Inuit Suicide Prevention Strategy](https://itk.ca/projects/national-inuit-suicide-prevention-strategy/)

No mortality rate is displayed. Do not present Nunavut as all Inuit Nunangat or imply that existing support is absent. The Iqaluit photo provides geographical context, not evidence about the wellbeing of people pictured or Neorons activity there.

## Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples — Australia

**Anchor:** latitude −25.3, longitude 133.8.

The displayed **34.8 per 100,000** is the **2025 age-standardised suicide rate** for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people across **New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia, Western Australia, and the Northern Territory**. Tasmania and the Australian Capital Territory are excluded. This is preliminary registration data and may be revised. It is not a rate specific to Waiben, the place shown in the photo.

- [Australian Bureau of Statistics: Intentional self-harm (suicide) deaths, 2025](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/health/causes-death/intentional-self-harm-suicide-deaths/2025), table “Suicide of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, 2025,” six-jurisdiction total, SDR column.
- [National Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Suicide Prevention Strategy 2025–2035](https://www.health.gov.au/resources/publications/national-aboriginal-and-torres-strait-islander-suicide-prevention-strategy)
- [Strategy PDF](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-12/national-aboriginal-and-torres-strait-islander-suicide-prevention-strategy.pdf), especially priorities 4 and 5 on printed pages 19–20.

The strategy was developed with Gayaa Dhuwi (Proud Spirit) Australia. It supports the page’s emphasis on culturally safe care, community-controlled services, and an Indigenous workforce. These are improvement priorities, not a claim that no services exist. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples comprise many distinct communities and cultures. Avoid trend claims without accounting for changes in Indigenous identification in mortality data.

## Māori — Aotearoa New Zealand

**Anchor:** latitude −39.0, longitude 176.0.

The displayed **28.1 per 100,000** concerns **Māori males and suspected suicide in 2024/25**. The same report gives 14.2 per 100,000 for non-Māori males. These are provisional suspected deaths rather than confirmed coronial findings. The figure is not an all-Māori combined-sex rate or a rate specific to Ōhinemutu.

- [Ministry of Health: Health and Independence Report 2025](https://www.health.govt.nz/publications/health-and-independence-report-2025-online-version), suicide section.
- [Suicide Prevention Action Plan 2025–2029](https://www.health.govt.nz/publications/suicide-prevention-action-plan-2025-2029)
- [Action Plan PDF](https://www.health.govt.nz/system/files/2025-09/suicide-prevention-action-plan-2025-2029-v2.pdf), printed page 4 for consultation themes and printed page 6 for existing community programmes.

Consultation identified a need for more kaupapa Māori approaches and broader prevention and recovery support. The plan describes Kia Piki te Ora as promoting community wellbeing and resilience. It separately describes the Māori Suicide Prevention Community Programme as strengthening whānau, hapū, and iwi capacity; do not conflate those programme descriptions. Existing Māori-led work should remain visible alongside the access needs.

## Sámi communities — Norwegian Sápmi

**Anchor:** latitude 69.0, longitude 23.5.

The Sámi Parliament’s article, published February 9, 2026 and updated September 4, 2026, describes a project with SANKS to examine local suicide prevention and its fit with Sámi language and culture. It names Tana, Kautokeino, Hamarøy, and Røros as participating municipalities. English website copy paraphrases this Norwegian primary source.

- [Sámi Parliament: Selvmordsforebygging i Sápmi](https://sametinget.no/aktuelt/selvmordsforebygging-i-sapmi.45471.aspx)

No numerical mortality estimate is displayed. The source concerns a Norwegian initiative, not the entirety of Sápmi across national borders. It also describes existing support, so an assertion of no local or culturally specific services would contradict it. The Kárášjohka photograph illustrates place and institutions, not a local death rate.

## Shared boundaries

- Different years, population definitions, reporting coverage, and methods prevent a global ranking. Markers must not encode a league table of mortality rates.
- Keep the year, sex/population, geography, and provisional status with each figure. Sources are linked for further reading; material qualifiers should not be hidden solely inside source disclosures.
- National crisis services can coexist with gaps in language, local access, cultural safety, and continuity. This is a synthesis of the sources, not a claim of an identical gap in every place.
- Indigenous identity is not presented as the cause of suicide. Community strengths, structural conditions, access, language, and local leadership are part of the context.
- Regional photographs are contextual imagery. They do not depict Neorons programmes, identify people as experiencing suicidal distress, or establish that a photographed location has a particular rate.
- The access-design section describes priorities for future collaboration. It must not promise clinically effective AI care, crisis intervention, diagnosis, validated Indigenous-language coverage, current community governance, or working referral arrangements without programme-specific evidence.
- The illustrative phone conversation is not a live service demonstration. AI conversations do not replace professional care or emergency services.
- This material is research context, not emergency-service routing. No hotline numbers or direct hotline links were added.

Public atlas summaries are brief paraphrases, well below 200 derived words per source. No extended quotations are used. Source notes document the same factual scope rather than adding new public claims.
